I.

About

I am a Ph.D. candidate in economics at George Mason University, where I also earned my M.A. in 2025. Before Mason, I studied economics at the University of Connecticut. My research sits in political economy and institutional theory, and returns to one question: how do people build peaceful, cooperative societies without depending on the coercive power of the state?

My dissertation develops a bottom-up theory of peace, tracing how relational goods and shared expectations scale outward — from the family, to the community, to relationships between strangers and trading partners. A related line of work, with Christopher Coyne, looks at how crises reshape the powers governments claim for themselves, using the Defense Production Act as a case study.

I am a Ph.D. Fellow with the F.A. Hayek Program for Advanced Study in Politics, Philosophy, and Economics and a fellow with the Initiative for the Study of a Stable Peace, both at George Mason's Mercatus Center. Alongside my academic work, I write for a general audience at the Foundation for Economic Education.

Teaching

I teach principles-level and elective courses in economics, built around real-world examples rather than heavy formalism. My aim is for students who have never taken an economics course to leave the semester noticing trade-offs and unintended consequences in ordinary life, not just on an exam.
